FARM COST PLANNING

Understanding Farm Costs in Kenya

Knowing how much it costs to produce a crop is an important part of farm planning. Production expenses can vary depending on the crop, location, farm size, input prices, labour requirements and production methods.

Keeping accurate records helps farmers understand where money is being spent and makes it easier to estimate revenue, profitability and cost per acre.

FARM EXPENSES

Common Farm Cost Categories

Agricultural production can involve many different expenses. Organising them into categories makes farm budgeting easier.

🌱 Seed

Record the cost of seed or planting material used for each crop and production area.

🧪 Fertilizer

Include fertilizer, manure and other soil fertility inputs used during production.

👨‍🌾 Labour

Labour costs may include land preparation, planting, weeding, spraying, harvesting and other farm tasks.

🐛 Crop Protection

Record expenses related to pest, disease and weed management products and application.

🚜 Machinery

Machinery expenses may include tractor hire, equipment hire, fuel and other operational costs.

🧺 Harvesting

Include harvesting, threshing, sorting, drying, packaging and related post-harvest expenses.

FARM BUDGET

Build a Simple Farm Budget

A basic farm budget can start with a list of expected production expenses. Estimate the quantity and price of each input, then add the costs together.

For example, a maize budget could include land preparation, seed, fertilizer, labour, weed management, crop protection, harvesting and transport.

Actual costs will vary between farms and seasons, so estimates should be based on current local prices and the farmer's own production plan.

COST PER ACRE

Understanding Cost per Acre

Cost per acre helps farmers compare production expenses across different fields, crops or seasons.

1. Calculate Total Costs

Add all relevant production expenses for the farm activity.

2. Determine Area

Record the actual area used for production, preferably using a consistent unit such as acres or hectares.

3. Calculate Cost per Acre

Divide total production costs by the number of acres cultivated.

FARM PROFITABILITY

From Farm Costs to Profit

Farm profitability depends on both production costs and revenue. Revenue can be estimated from the quantity sold and the selling price.

A simple profit calculation is:

Estimated Profit

Revenue − Total Production Costs = Estimated Profit

This calculation is a simplified planning measure. Farmers should consider all relevant costs and income when assessing the financial performance of a farming activity.

FARM RECORDS

Keep Better Production Records

📝 Record Every Expense

Keep receipts, notes or digital records of inputs, labour and other production expenses.

📅 Record Dates

Note when inputs were purchased and when important farm activities were completed.

⚖️ Record Harvest

Record harvested quantities so production can be compared with the costs incurred.

💰 Record Selling Prices

Keep track of quantities sold and the prices received to estimate actual farm revenue.
