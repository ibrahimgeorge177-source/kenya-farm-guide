FARM COSTS & BUDGETING

Understand the Cost of Farming

Learn how to organize farm expenses, estimate production costs, calculate cost per acre and understand the relationship between farm costs, revenue and profit.

Why Farm Cost Records Matter

Knowing how much money goes into producing a crop can help a farmer understand whether a farming activity is financially sustainable.

Farm costs are not limited to seed and fertilizer. Land preparation, labour, crop protection, harvesting, transport, storage and other expenses can all contribute to the final cost of production.

Costs also vary between farms and seasons. The figures used in a farm budget should therefore be based on the farmer's actual situation whenever possible.

Common Farm Cost Categories

🚜

Land Preparation

This can include ploughing, harrowing, land clearing and other activities required before planting.

🌱

Seed and Planting Material

Include the cost of seed, planting materials and any related treatment or preparation costs.

🧪

Fertilizer and Soil Inputs

Record fertilizer, manure, soil amendments and other inputs used to support crop production.

👨‍🌾

Labour

Labour costs may include planting, weeding, spraying, harvesting and other farm activities.

🛡️

Crop Protection

Keep records of approved crop protection products and the labour or application costs associated with them.

🌾

Harvesting and Post-Harvest

Include harvesting, shelling, drying, sorting, storage, packaging and other post-harvest expenses.

How to Create a Simple Farm Budget

A farm budget can start with a simple list of expected expenses. Organize the costs according to the activities involved in producing the crop.

  1. Identify the crop and size of land to be cultivated.
  2. List the seed or planting materials required.
  3. Estimate land preparation and planting costs.
  4. Estimate fertilizer, manure and crop protection costs.
  5. Include labour costs for major farm activities.
  6. Estimate harvesting, transport and post-harvest costs.
  7. Add the individual expenses to estimate total production cost.

Understanding Cost Per Acre

Total Cost

Add all relevant production expenses to determine the estimated total cost of the farming activity.

Farm Area

Record the actual area cultivated. This allows total costs to be compared between farms of different sizes.

Cost Per Acre

Cost per acre can be estimated by dividing total production cost by the number of acres cultivated.

From Farm Cost to Farm Profit

Farm profit depends on both production costs and the revenue generated from the harvested crop.

A simple estimate can be expressed as:

Estimated Profit = Estimated Revenue − Total Production Cost

For example, if a farmer estimates revenue of KSh 90,000 and total production costs of KSh 35,000, the estimated profit would be KSh 55,000.

This is only an example. Actual farm returns depend on production, selling price, quality, losses and the costs incurred by the individual farmer.

Keep Better Farm Records

Good records make it easier to review a farming season after harvest. Instead of relying on memory, record expenses when they occur.

  • Record the date of each major expense.
  • Record the quantity purchased.
  • Record the price paid.
  • Record labour and machinery costs.
  • Record the final harvested quantity.
  • Record the selling price and total revenue.

These records can then be used to compare seasons and identify where production costs are increasing.
