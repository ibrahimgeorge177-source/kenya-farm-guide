Understanding Farm Costs in Kenya

Good farm planning starts with knowing what you spend. Learn how to organise production costs, estimate cost per acre and use your numbers when planning expected returns.

Use Farm Calculators Explore Cost Categories

Why Farm Cost Planning Matters

A farm can produce a good harvest and still have limited returns if production costs are not properly understood. Recording expenses helps you see where money is going and makes it easier to plan future seasons.

Know Your Spending

Record what you spend on inputs, labour, transport and other activities instead of relying only on memory.

Compare Costs

Looking at costs per acre can help you understand how much different production activities require.

Plan Ahead

Previous farm records can provide useful information when preparing a budget for the next production cycle.

Common Farm Cost Categories

The exact costs vary by crop, location, farm size, production system and season.

Seed

Include the cost of seed or planting material required for the cultivated area.

Record the quantity purchased, price and crop variety where possible.

Fertilizer & Inputs

Include fertilizer, manure and other crop inputs used during land preparation and production.

Keeping quantities and prices separately can make future budgeting easier.

Labour

Labour may include land preparation, planting, weeding, spraying, harvesting and post-harvest work.

Record both hired labour and other labour costs that you want included in your farm analysis.

Machinery & Equipment

Depending on the farm, costs can include tractor services, machinery hire, equipment use, fuel and maintenance.

Transport

Consider transport for inputs, harvested produce and other farm-related activities.

Other Farm Expenses

Other costs may include irrigation, storage, packaging, crop protection, land preparation and other activities relevant to the farm.

A Simple Farm Budgeting Process

You can start with a simple record and make it more detailed as your farm management improves.

1. Define the Farm Activity

Identify the crop, cultivated area and production period you are budgeting for.

2. List Expected Expenses

Write down the inputs, labour, machinery, transport and other expenses you expect to incur.

3. Record Actual Spending

As the season progresses, replace estimates with actual amounts paid whenever possible.

4. Calculate the Total

Add the different expense categories to understand the total production cost.

5. Calculate Cost Per Acre

Divide total production cost by cultivated area to understand the average cost per acre.

6. Review the Results

Compare planned and actual costs and use the records to improve future farm planning.

Understanding Cost Per Acre

Cost per acre provides a simple way to understand how much money is being spent relative to the area under production.

The basic calculation is:

Cost Per Acre

Total Production Cost ÷ Cultivated Area

For example, if a farming activity costs KES 100,000 and covers 5 acres, the average production cost is KES 20,000 per acre.

This is a simple average. Actual spending can vary between fields or parts of a farm depending on the activities and inputs used.

Connecting Costs to Farm Returns

Production costs are only one part of understanding the economics of a farming activity.

Production

Estimate how much produce you expect to harvest based on the crop, area and production conditions.

Revenue

Expected revenue can be estimated by multiplying the quantity sold by the selling price.

Remaining Amount

Subtracting production costs from expected revenue gives a basic estimate of the amount remaining after those costs.

Keep Better Farm Records

A simple farm record can make cost analysis much easier. You do not necessarily need complicated software to get started.

Useful information to record

  • Date of the expense
  • Item or service purchased
  • Quantity
  • Price paid
  • Farm activity or field involved
  • Labour or service provider where relevant
  • Harvest quantity
  • Selling price and quantity sold

Keeping these records throughout the season is generally more useful than trying to reconstruct all expenses after harvesting.
