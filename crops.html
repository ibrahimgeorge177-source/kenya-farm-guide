Understanding Crop Farming

Farmers in Kenya grow a wide range of crops for food, household use, livestock needs and income. The crops that perform well on a particular farm can depend on factors such as rainfall, soil conditions, altitude, available water, market access and farm management.

Good crop planning starts before planting. Farmers can consider the expected production period, input requirements, available labour, production costs and how the harvest will be used or sold.

The guides on this page are designed to provide practical starting points for farmers and people learning about agriculture.

Things to Consider Before Planting

Know Your Farm

Consider soil type, rainfall patterns, available water, altitude and the amount of land available before choosing a crop.

Calculate Input Costs

Estimate the cost of seed, fertilizer, labour, crop protection, land preparation, harvesting and transport before starting production.

Consider the Market

Think about where the crop will be sold, expected demand, storage options and transport costs before committing resources to production.

Crop Diversification

Growing different crops can give a farm more than one source of food or income. Farmers may also use different crops to spread production across different seasons or markets.

The most suitable combination depends on local conditions, available resources and the farmer's goals. Before introducing a new crop, it is useful to consider its production requirements and potential market.

Intercropping is another approach where compatible crops are grown together on the same piece of land. Proper planning is important because crops can compete for light, water, nutrients and space.
