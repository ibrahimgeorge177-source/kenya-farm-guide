DIGITAL AGRICULTURE

Using Technology to Make Farming More Practical

Agricultural technology can help farmers collect better information, plan farm activities, monitor conditions and make more informed decisions. This guide explains practical ways technology can support farming in Kenya.

Use Farm Calculators Explore Technology

WHY IT MATTERS

Technology Does Not Have to Be Complicated

Farm technology can range from something as simple as keeping digital records on a phone to more advanced systems that use weather data, sensors, satellite information or artificial intelligence.

Better Farm Records

Digital records can make it easier to track planting dates, input purchases, labour, production, sales and other farm activities.

Better Planning

Simple digital tools can help farmers estimate costs, production, revenue and break-even points before making important farm decisions.

Better Information

Weather information, crop information and farm data can help farmers understand changing conditions and plan activities more carefully.

KEY AREAS

Useful Agricultural Technologies

Different technologies solve different farm problems. The most useful solution is often the one that is simple enough to use consistently and provides information that can support a real decision.

1. Digital Farm Records

Farmers can use phones, spreadsheets or other digital tools to record farm activities and expenses.

  • Seed purchases
  • Fertilizer and input costs
  • Labour expenses
  • Planting and harvesting dates
  • Production and sales

2. Weather Information

Weather information can support decisions around planting, field activities, crop protection and harvesting.

  • Rainfall information
  • Temperature
  • Weather forecasts
  • Dry and wet periods
  • Seasonal planning

3. Farm Calculators

Digital calculators can turn basic farm information into useful estimates for planning.

  • Expected crop yield
  • Production costs
  • Cost per acre
  • Revenue and profit
  • Break-even price

4. Data and AI Tools

Data-driven systems can help identify patterns in farm information and support planning or risk assessment.

  • Yield analysis
  • Farm performance tracking
  • Risk identification
  • Data-based recommendations
  • Decision support

5. Precision Agriculture

Precision agriculture uses information about different parts of a farm to improve how resources are managed.

  • Soil information
  • Field mapping
  • Satellite imagery
  • Sensors
  • Variable farm conditions

6. Digital Market Information

Digital platforms can help farmers access information about markets, buyers, prices and opportunities.

  • Market research
  • Price comparisons
  • Finding potential buyers
  • Planning sales
  • Tracking farm income

PRACTICAL EXAMPLE

How Technology Can Support a Farming Decision

Planning a Maize Crop

Imagine a farmer preparing to plant maize on several acres. Instead of relying only on memory, the farmer can combine several simple sources of information.

Step 1: Estimate the Farm Requirements

The farmer records the cultivated area and estimates the amount of seed, fertilizer and labour required.

Step 2: Estimate Production Costs

Expected input and labour expenses can be entered into a farm cost calculator to estimate the total production cost and cost per acre.

Step 3: Consider Expected Revenue

An expected production quantity and selling price can be used to estimate potential revenue and the amount remaining after production costs.

Step 4: Monitor the Season

During the season, the farmer can continue recording expenses, field activities, weather observations and production information.

Step 5: Review the Results

At the end of the season, the records provide a clearer picture of what was spent, what was produced and which activities may need to change in the next season.

START SMALL

Simple Technology Can Still Make a Difference

Farmers do not need expensive equipment or complex software to start using digital tools. A practical approach is to begin with one problem and introduce technology where it provides clear value.

Start With Records

Record every major farm expense and activity. This creates useful information for understanding where money is being spent.

Use Information Before Acting

Check available crop, weather, market and farm information before making decisions rather than relying on a single source.

Review What Worked

Compare planned costs and activities with what actually happened. Good records become more valuable with each farming season.

SMART USE

Use Digital Tools as Decision Support

Technology should support practical farming knowledge rather than replace it. Calculators provide estimates, forecasts can change, and data-driven recommendations depend on the quality of the information used.

A useful rule for farmers

Treat digital information as one part of the decision-making process. Compare it with field conditions, local experience, reliable agricultural guidance and the actual circumstances of the farm.
