How Technology Is Changing Agriculture

Farming involves many decisions, from choosing when to plant and what inputs to use to estimating production and deciding when and where to sell a harvest.

Digital technology can help organize information and make some of these decisions easier to understand. Farmers can use weather information, farm records, mobile tools, calculators, mapping technologies and data-driven systems alongside their own experience and local knowledge.

Technology is most useful when it supports practical farm decisions rather than replacing local knowledge and professional agricultural advice.

Using Data in Agriculture

Farm data can include planting dates, seed varieties, fertilizer applications, labour costs, rainfall observations, harvested quantities and selling prices.

When these records are kept consistently, farmers can compare different seasons and identify patterns in their production.

Example: If a farmer records production and input costs for several seasons, the records can help show how changes in costs, production or selling prices affected the farm.

Data does not automatically provide the answer to every farming problem. Its value depends on the quality of the information collected and how it is interpreted.

Artificial Intelligence in Agriculture

Artificial intelligence and machine learning can be used to identify patterns in agricultural data and support certain types of decision-making.

Possible applications include crop classification, disease detection, yield estimation, weather analysis, recommendation systems and agricultural risk assessment.

For example, a machine-learning system could analyze historical agricultural data and estimate expected crop production under particular conditions.

Such systems should be treated as decision-support tools. Their predictions depend on the quality and relevance of the data used to build them.

Digital Tools for Farmers

Digital tools can help simplify everyday farm activities. Examples include calculators, farm-management software, mobile applications, online agricultural information services and digital marketplaces.

Even a simple calculator can be useful when it replaces repeated manual calculations and makes assumptions clear.

The most useful tools are usually those that solve a specific problem and are simple enough to use consistently.

Weather and Farming Decisions

Weather conditions influence many agricultural activities. Rainfall and temperature can affect planting, crop growth, disease pressure, harvesting and post-harvest operations.

Farmers can use available weather information together with observations from their own farms when planning activities.

Important: Weather forecasts are estimates and conditions can change. Farmers should consider local conditions and appropriate agricultural guidance when making important production decisions.

Precision Agriculture

Precision agriculture uses information about specific locations or parts of a farm to help understand variation within agricultural land.

Technologies such as GPS, satellite imagery, sensors and field mapping can provide information about differences in crops, soil or farm conditions.

This information can potentially help farmers identify areas that require closer attention or different management.

Digital Farm Records

Moving farm records into a spreadsheet, mobile application or other digital system can make information easier to organize and review.

Useful records can include:

  • Crop and variety planted
  • Planting date
  • Land area
  • Seed quantity and cost
  • Fertilizer and other input costs
  • Labour expenses
  • Harvest quantity
  • Selling price and revenue

Over time, these records can become a valuable source of information for evaluating farm performance.
