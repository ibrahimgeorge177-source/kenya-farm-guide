Potato Farming in Kenya: A Practical Guide

Irish potatoes are an important food and commercial crop in Kenya. Potato production can provide farmers with food for the household as well as produce for local and commercial markets.

Successful potato production depends on several factors, including quality planting material, suitable soil, moisture, crop nutrition, weed management, pest and disease monitoring, harvesting and post-harvest handling.

Conditions vary between potato-growing areas, so production decisions should be adapted to the local environment and the variety being grown.

1. Start With Quality Seed

Planting material is one of the most important decisions in potato production.

Farmers should obtain suitable, healthy planting material from reliable sources rather than assuming that any stored potato will perform well as seed.

When planning seed purchases, consider:

  • The variety
  • Seed quality and health
  • Expected maturity period
  • Market requirements
  • Availability of suitable planting material
  • Local growing conditions

Keep a record of the variety and quantity purchased so that production results can be compared between seasons.

2. Choose a Suitable Field

Potato fields should be assessed before planting.

Soil condition, drainage, previous crops and field history can influence crop performance.

Pay particular attention to:

  • Soil structure
  • Drainage
  • Soil fertility
  • Previous crop history
  • Weed pressure
  • Potential pest and disease problems

Avoid making field decisions based only on how productive the land appears. Good records and soil information can provide a stronger basis for planning.

3. Prepare the Soil Properly

Potato tubers develop underground, making suitable soil conditions particularly important.

Land preparation should aim to provide a suitable environment for root and tuber development while avoiding unnecessary soil disturbance.

The appropriate method will depend on soil type, previous land use, available equipment and local farming practices.

4. Plan Planting Around Soil Moisture

Potatoes require adequate moisture for establishment and crop development.

For rain-fed production, planting decisions should take account of the expected rainfall pattern and available soil moisture.

Farmers should avoid treating a calendar date as a universal planting recommendation because conditions differ between regions and seasons.

Where irrigation is available, water management can provide greater control, but irrigation should still be planned according to crop needs and local conditions.

5. Use Appropriate Spacing

Spacing affects plant population and the amount of space available for tuber development.

Recommended spacing can vary according to variety, seed size, production system and local recommendations.

Planning Factor Why It Matters
Row spacing Determines how plants are distributed across the field.
Plant spacing Influences plant population and competition.
Seed size Can influence planting requirements and establishment.
Field area Helps determine the amount of seed required.

6. Manage Soil Fertility

Potato production can place significant demands on soil nutrients, making nutrient management an important part of production planning.

Where practical, soil testing can help identify soil fertility conditions and guide nutrient-management decisions.

Farmers should consider soil information, previous cropping and local recommendations when selecting fertilizer or other soil inputs.

Applying more fertilizer than necessary does not automatically guarantee higher yields and may increase production costs or create other problems.

7. Control Weeds

Weeds compete with potato plants for water, nutrients, sunlight and growing space.

Weed management should therefore be planned early rather than waiting until weeds become difficult to control.

Regular field scouting allows farmers to identify weed problems and choose an appropriate control method.

8. Monitor for Pests

Potato crops can be affected by different insect pests depending on location, weather and production conditions.

Regular scouting is useful for identifying unusual feeding damage and determining whether further investigation is necessary.

Crop protection decisions should be based on correct identification of the problem and appropriate local agricultural guidance.

When using crop protection products, follow the product label, safety instructions and applicable agricultural requirements.

9. Monitor for Diseases

Potato diseases can affect leaves, stems, roots and tubers.

Disease risk can be influenced by weather, crop history, planting material, variety and field management.

Farmers should regularly inspect the crop for unusual symptoms such as leaf spots, discoloration, wilting or problems affecting tubers.

Correct diagnosis is important because different diseases and disorders require different management approaches.

10. Pay Attention to Hilling and Tuber Development

Soil management around developing potato plants is an important part of crop production.

Farmers should follow locally appropriate recommendations for ridging, hilling and other field operations used in their production system.

The aim is to provide suitable conditions for tuber development while supporting good field management.

11. Manage Water Carefully

Both insufficient and excessive moisture can create problems for potato production.

Moisture stress can affect crop development, while poorly drained soils can create unsuitable conditions around roots and tubers.

Farmers should monitor field conditions rather than relying only on rainfall totals.

Where irrigation is used, water application should be managed carefully according to crop and soil conditions.

12. Plan the Harvest

Harvesting should be planned around crop maturity, market requirements, available labour and expected weather conditions.

Before harvesting, farmers should consider:

  • Whether the crop has reached the intended maturity
  • Available harvesting labour
  • Storage arrangements
  • Transport requirements
  • Expected market conditions

13. Reduce Post-Harvest Losses

Potatoes can suffer losses after harvesting if they are poorly handled, exposed to unsuitable conditions or stored incorrectly.

Careful handling can reduce unnecessary physical damage.

Storage conditions should be appropriate for the intended storage period and market or household use.

Farmers should also keep harvested potatoes away from conditions that could encourage deterioration.

Estimating Potato Yield

Yield can be calculated by relating the harvested quantity to the area harvested.

Yield per acre = Total harvested quantity ÷ Area harvested

For example, if a farmer harvests 6,000 kg from 4 acres:

6,000 kg ÷ 4 acres = 1,500 kg per acre

Recording yield by field or production season can help identify changes in performance over time.

Understanding Potato Production Costs

Potato production can involve several expenses, including seed, land preparation, fertilizer, labour, crop protection, harvesting, transport and storage.

Farmers should record actual expenses rather than relying only on estimates.

Estimating Revenue and Profit

A productive crop does not necessarily mean that the farming enterprise generated a profit.

Revenue depends on the quantity sold and the price received, while profit also depends on total production costs.

Revenue = Quantity Sold × Selling Price

Profit = Revenue − Total Production Costs

Common Potato Farming Mistakes

  • Using poor-quality or unsuitable planting material.
  • Ignoring soil and drainage conditions.
  • Planting without considering available moisture.
  • Using unsuitable plant spacing.
  • Applying inputs without considering soil needs.
  • Allowing weeds to compete with the crop for too long.
  • Failing to monitor pests and diseases.
  • Ignoring post-harvest handling and storage.
  • Failing to record production costs.
  • Judging profitability from yield alone.

A Simple Potato Farming Checklist

  • Choose suitable and healthy planting material.
  • Assess the field before planting.
  • Prepare the soil appropriately.
  • Plan planting around local moisture conditions.
  • Use suitable spacing and seed quantities.
  • Manage soil fertility based on available information.
  • Monitor and control weeds.
  • Scout for pests and diseases.
  • Plan harvesting and storage before the crop matures.
  • Record yield, costs and sales.

Final Takeaway

Potato farming requires attention throughout the production cycle, from selecting planting material to managing the field and handling harvested tubers.

Farmers can improve their decision-making by combining agronomic observations with simple production and financial records.

Tracking the variety, planting conditions, inputs, crop problems, harvest quantity and selling price can provide useful information for planning future seasons.

Important:

This article provides general educational information. Recommended varieties, planting practices, fertilizer use, pest and disease management and irrigation requirements can vary by location and production system. Use locally appropriate agricultural guidance when making farm decisions.
