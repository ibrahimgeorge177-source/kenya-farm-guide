Sweet Potato Farming in Kenya: A Practical Guide

Sweet potatoes are an important food crop in Kenya and can also provide farmers with an opportunity to generate income from local markets.

Production involves more than planting vines and waiting for harvest. Farmers need to consider suitable planting material, soil preparation, planting time, moisture, weed management, pests, diseases and harvesting.

Sweet potato production practices can vary between regions and varieties, so farmers should adapt their decisions to local conditions.

1. Choose a Suitable Variety

Sweet potato varieties can differ in maturity, root characteristics, colour, taste, productivity and suitability for different environments and markets.

When selecting a variety, consider:

  • Local growing conditions
  • Expected maturity period
  • Consumer preferences
  • Market demand
  • Availability of healthy planting material
  • Previous production experience in the area

Keeping a record of the variety planted can help farmers compare results between seasons.

2. Use Healthy Planting Material

Sweet potatoes are commonly established using vines or other appropriate planting material rather than relying only on true botanical seed.

Planting material should be obtained from reliable sources and should be inspected for obvious signs of poor health.

Using unsuitable planting material can affect crop establishment and may introduce production problems into the field.

3. Prepare the Land

Good land preparation provides a suitable environment for root development.

The preparation method depends on soil type, previous crop, field conditions and the production system.

Before planting, assess the field for:

  • Soil structure
  • Drainage
  • Existing weeds
  • Soil fertility concerns
  • Previous crop history
  • Possible pest and disease problems

4. Consider Soil Conditions

Soil condition can influence root development, water availability and nutrient supply.

Where practical, soil testing can help farmers understand the fertility status of a field.

Soil management decisions should take into account the crop's requirements, previous cropping and available soil information.

5. Plant When Conditions Are Suitable

Planting should be planned around moisture availability and local seasonal conditions.

For rain-fed production, farmers should consider expected rainfall rather than relying on a fixed calendar date that may not suit every region.

Adequate moisture around establishment can support better crop establishment, while prolonged moisture stress can affect crop development.

6. Use Appropriate Spacing

Spacing affects plant population and the amount of growing space available to each plant.

The appropriate arrangement depends on the variety, production system and local recommendations.

Consideration Why It Matters
Row arrangement Determines how plants are distributed across the field.
Plant spacing Influences plant population and competition.
Planting material Healthy material supports better establishment.
Field size Helps determine the quantity of planting material required.

7. Manage Soil Fertility

Sweet potatoes require suitable nutrients for healthy plant growth and root development.

Farmers should avoid applying fertilizer simply because a crop is expected to benefit from more inputs.

Soil information, previous crop history and local agricultural recommendations should be considered when planning nutrient management.

Good soil management should also consider long-term soil health rather than focusing only on one harvest.

8. Control Weeds

Weeds compete with sweet potato plants for water, nutrients, light and space.

Early weed management can help reduce competition while the crop is becoming established.

Farmers should monitor the field regularly and use suitable weed management methods for their production system.

9. Monitor for Pests

Sweet potato crops can be affected by different pests, with the level of pressure varying between locations and seasons.

Regular scouting helps farmers identify unusual damage and investigate the likely cause.

Crop protection decisions should be based on correct identification of the problem and appropriate local recommendations.

If crop protection products are used, farmers should follow the product label and all relevant safety requirements.

10. Watch for Diseases

Diseases can affect sweet potato plants and may reduce crop performance under suitable conditions for disease development.

Farmers should inspect vines and leaves regularly for unusual symptoms such as spots, discoloration, wilting or abnormal growth.

Correct identification is important because similar symptoms can sometimes have different causes.

11. Manage Moisture

Moisture availability can influence crop establishment and root development.

Farmers should monitor soil moisture, especially during periods of limited rainfall.

At the same time, excessive water and poor drainage can create unsuitable soil conditions.

Where irrigation is available, water management should be based on crop requirements, soil conditions and local weather.

12. Monitor Root Development

Sweet potato production ultimately depends on the development of storage roots.

Field observations can help farmers understand whether the crop is developing normally.

Root development can be influenced by soil conditions, moisture, nutrients, variety and general crop management.

13. Plan Harvesting

Harvest timing should consider variety, maturity, intended use, market requirements and soil conditions.

Farmers should plan labour, tools, transport and market arrangements before harvesting begins.

Careful harvesting can also help reduce physical damage to roots.

14. Handle the Harvest Carefully

Sweet potato roots can be damaged during digging, sorting, transportation and storage.

Farmers should handle harvested roots carefully and separate damaged or unsuitable produce where appropriate.

Storage conditions should be suitable for the intended storage period and end use.

Estimating Sweet Potato Yield

Farmers can calculate yield by relating the harvested quantity to the area harvested.

Yield per acre = Total harvested quantity ÷ Area harvested

For example, if a farmer harvests 4,500 kg from 3 acres:

4,500 kg ÷ 3 acres = 1,500 kg per acre

Using the same units and measurement approach makes it easier to compare results between seasons.

Understanding Production Costs

Sweet potato production can involve costs for land preparation, planting material, labour, soil inputs, crop protection, harvesting and transport.

Farmers should record both cash expenses and, where useful, important farm resources such as family labour.

Estimating Revenue and Profit

Yield and profit are not the same thing.

Revenue depends on the quantity sold and the selling price, while profit also accounts for production expenses.

Revenue = Quantity Sold × Selling Price

Profit = Revenue − Total Production Costs

Selling prices can change between markets and seasons, so farmers should record actual prices received whenever possible.

Sweet Potato as Part of a Diversified Farm

Sweet potatoes can be included alongside other crops as part of a diversified farming system.

Crop diversification can allow farmers to spread production across different crops and markets, although each crop still has its own production and market risks.

Common Sweet Potato Farming Mistakes

  • Using unsuitable or poor-quality planting material.
  • Ignoring soil and drainage conditions.
  • Planting without considering available moisture.
  • Using unsuitable spacing.
  • Applying inputs without considering crop and soil needs.
  • Allowing weeds to compete with the crop for too long.
  • Failing to scout for pests and diseases.
  • Harvesting without planning labour and transport.
  • Handling roots roughly after harvest.
  • Failing to keep production and sales records.

A Simple Sweet Potato Farming Checklist

  • Choose a suitable variety.
  • Use healthy planting material.
  • Prepare the field appropriately.
  • Consider soil and moisture conditions.
  • Use suitable spacing.
  • Manage soil fertility appropriately.
  • Control weeds early.
  • Scout for pests and diseases.
  • Plan harvesting and post-harvest handling.
  • Record yield, costs and sales.

Final Takeaway

Sweet potato farming requires attention from planting material selection through field management, harvesting and marketing.

Farmers can make better decisions by combining observations from the field with reliable production and financial records.

Tracking variety, planting conditions, inputs, crop problems, harvested quantity and selling prices provides useful information for planning future seasons.

Important:

This article provides general educational information. Recommended varieties, planting practices, nutrient requirements, pest and disease management and harvesting practices can vary by location and production system. Use locally appropriate agricultural guidance when making farm decisions.
