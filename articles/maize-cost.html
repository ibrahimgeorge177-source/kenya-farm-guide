Maize Farming Costs in Kenya

Understanding the cost of maize production is an important part of farm planning. Farmers may spend money on land preparation, seed, fertilizer, labour, crop protection, harvesting, transport and other activities.

Actual costs vary depending on location, farm size, production methods, input prices, labour rates and the services available to the farmer.

Main Costs in Maize Production

A maize farm budget should include the major expenses expected during the production cycle.

Land Preparation

Land preparation may involve ploughing, harrowing, planting preparation and other activities needed before the crop is established. The cost depends on whether the work is done manually, using hired machinery or through other arrangements.

Seed

Seed is an important production input. The total cost depends on the variety selected, quantity required and price paid by the farmer.

Fertilizer and Soil Amendments

Fertilizer and other soil inputs can form a significant part of production costs. The appropriate type and amount should take into account soil conditions and local agricultural recommendations.

Labour

Labour costs may include land preparation, planting, weeding, fertilizer application, crop protection, harvesting and post-harvest activities.

Weed, Pest and Disease Management

Farmers may incur costs for herbicides, pesticides, fungicides, application services or manual crop management. The actual requirement depends on field conditions and pest or disease pressure.

Harvesting

Harvesting expenses can include labour, shelling, drying, sorting, storage preparation and other post-harvest activities.

Transport

Transport may be required for moving inputs to the farm and transporting harvested maize to a storage facility, market or buyer.

How to Prepare a Maize Farm Budget

A simple farm budget can help a farmer estimate how much money may be required before production begins.

  1. Estimate the area you intend to cultivate.
  2. List the seed and other inputs required.
  3. Estimate the quantity and cost of fertilizer.
  4. List expected labour activities and their costs.
  5. Include crop protection and other farm expenses.
  6. Estimate harvesting and post-harvest costs.
  7. Include expected transport and storage expenses.
  8. Add the individual expenses to estimate total cost.

Keeping these figures in one record makes it easier to compare the estimated budget with actual spending after the season.

Cost per Acre

Farmers can calculate average production cost per acre by dividing total farm production costs by the area cultivated.

Cost per acre = Total production cost ÷ Area cultivated

For example, if total production expenses are KSh 40,000 and the farmer cultivates 2 acres, the average production cost is KSh 20,000 per acre.

Understanding Maize Profit

Production cost alone does not determine whether a maize farm makes a profit. Revenue also depends on the quantity sold and the selling price.

A basic calculation is:

Revenue = Quantity sold × Selling price

Estimated profit can then be calculated as:

Profit = Revenue − Total production cost

Actual profitability can also be affected by storage costs, transport, post-harvest losses, market changes and other expenses.

Why Farmers Should Keep Cost Records

Recording farm expenses provides a clearer picture of how money is being spent during the production season.

  • Track spending on different inputs.
  • Identify major production expenses.
  • Compare estimated and actual costs.
  • Calculate cost per acre.
  • Estimate revenue and profit.
  • Improve planning for future seasons.

Costs Can Change Between Seasons

Farmers should avoid treating a single farm budget as a fixed price list. Seed, fertilizer, labour, transport and other agricultural costs can change over time and may differ between locations.

Before planting, farmers should check current local prices and use quotations or actual market information where possible.
