How to Estimate Maize Yield per Acre in Kenya

Maize yield is one of the most useful measurements a farmer can keep when evaluating a crop. It allows you to compare production between different fields, seasons and farming practices.

Yield is normally expressed as the amount of harvested grain produced from a specific area of land, such as kilograms per acre or tonnes per hectare.

The calculation itself is simple. The important part is keeping reasonably accurate records of both the harvested grain and the area from which it was harvested.

The Basic Maize Yield Formula

To estimate maize yield per acre, divide the total harvested grain by the area harvested.

Formula:

Yield per acre = Total harvested grain (kg) ÷ Area harvested (acres)

Example: 2,000 kg from 2 Acres

Suppose a farmer harvests 2,000 kg of maize from a field measuring 2 acres.

The calculation would be:

Measurement Value
Total harvested grain 2,000 kg
Area harvested 2 acres
Estimated yield 1,000 kg per acre

Therefore:

2,000 kg ÷ 2 acres = 1,000 kg per acre

Converting Maize Bags into Kilograms

Farmers may record their harvest in bags rather than kilograms. Before calculating yield, you need to know the approximate weight of the bags being used.

For example, if a farmer has 20 bags and each bag contains 90 kg:

20 bags × 90 kg = 1,800 kg

If those 1,800 kg were harvested from 2 acres:

1,800 kg ÷ 2 acres = 900 kg per acre

The important point is to use the actual weight of the bags rather than assuming that every bag contains the same amount of maize.

Why Accurate Harvest Records Matter

Yield estimates are only as useful as the records behind them. A farmer who records harvest quantities and field sizes can build a much clearer picture of farm performance over time.

Useful records can include:

  • Total kilograms harvested
  • Number of bags harvested
  • Approximate weight per bag
  • Area harvested
  • Harvest date
  • Crop variety
  • Major inputs used
  • Major labour expenses
  • Important weather or pest observations

Yield per Acre vs Total Harvest

Total harvest and yield per acre are not the same measurement.

A farmer with a larger farm can have a larger total harvest while producing less grain per acre than a farmer with a smaller, more productive field.

Farm Area Total Harvest Yield
Farm A 2 acres 2,000 kg 1,000 kg/acre
Farm B 5 acres 4,000 kg 800 kg/acre

Farm B produces more maize in total, but Farm A has the higher yield per acre in this example.

What Can Affect Maize Yield?

Yield is influenced by many interacting factors. These can include the quality and suitability of seed, soil conditions, rainfall, planting time, plant population, weed competition, pests, diseases, nutrient availability and crop management.

Harvest and post-harvest losses can also affect the amount of grain that ultimately remains available for sale or consumption.

This means that comparing yields between two farms should be done carefully. Differences in location, weather, soil and management can make direct comparisons misleading.

Yield Does Not Tell the Whole Story

A high yield does not automatically mean that a farming enterprise generated a high profit.

A farmer may achieve a good harvest but also have high costs for seed, fertilizer, labour, land preparation, transport or other activities.

For this reason, yield should ideally be considered alongside farm costs and selling prices.

Practical Record-Keeping Tip

Instead of waiting until the end of the season to estimate production, keep records throughout the harvest period.

Record each batch or group of bags harvested and note the area associated with the production. If the crop comes from different fields, keep the records separate where possible.

Over time, these records can help you identify which fields, seasons or management approaches have produced different results.

Final Takeaway

Estimating maize yield per acre is straightforward: divide the total harvested grain in kilograms by the number of acres harvested.

The calculation is simple, but consistent record keeping makes the result much more useful. When yield records are combined with information about farm costs, weather and management practices, they can provide a stronger basis for understanding farm performance.

Remember:

Yield figures are estimates based on the measurements available. Actual farm performance can vary because of weather, soil, management, pests, diseases, post-harvest losses and other conditions.
