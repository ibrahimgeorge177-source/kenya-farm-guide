How to Estimate Maize Farming Profit in Kenya

Growing maize can involve significant spending before any income is received. Farmers may pay for land preparation, seed, fertilizer, labour, crop protection, harvesting, transport and other activities.

For this reason, looking only at the amount of maize harvested does not provide a complete picture of farm performance.

A simple profit estimate combines three important measurements: the amount produced, the price received and the total cost of production.

The Basic Profit Formula

A simple estimate of farm profit can be calculated using:

Profit = Revenue − Total Production Costs

Revenue can be estimated using:

Revenue = Quantity Sold × Selling Price

These calculations provide an estimate. Actual financial results can differ because of losses, changing prices, additional expenses and other farm conditions.

Example: Estimating Revenue

Suppose a farmer sells 2,000 kg of maize at KSh 45 per kilogram.

2,000 kg × KSh 45 = KSh 90,000

The estimated revenue in this example would therefore be KSh 90,000.

This is revenue, not profit. Production costs still need to be subtracted.

Adding Production Costs

Suppose the farmer's total production costs are KSh 60,000.

KSh 90,000 − KSh 60,000 = KSh 30,000

The estimated profit would therefore be KSh 30,000 in this simplified example.

Main Costs to Include

A realistic maize budget should include as many relevant costs as possible.

Cost Examples
Land preparation Ploughing, harrowing and related field work
Seed Seed purchased for planting
Fertilizer Basal fertilizer, top dressing and soil inputs
Labour Planting, weeding and other field activities
Crop protection Pest and disease management
Harvesting Harvest labour, shelling and related activities
Transport Moving inputs or harvested maize

Yield Has a Major Effect on Revenue

If the selling price remains the same, producing more marketable grain generally increases potential revenue.

For example, consider two simplified scenarios using the same selling price of KSh 45 per kilogram.

Scenario Harvest Price Revenue
A 1,500 kg KSh 45/kg KSh 67,500
B 2,000 kg KSh 45/kg KSh 90,000

However, higher production may also require additional costs. Therefore, revenue should always be considered together with the cost of producing the crop.

Why Selling Price Matters

Market price can have a major effect on farm revenue.

The same quantity of maize can generate different revenue at different selling prices.

Quantity Price Revenue
2,000 kg KSh 35/kg KSh 70,000
2,000 kg KSh 45/kg KSh 90,000
2,000 kg KSh 55/kg KSh 110,000

This illustrates why farmers should record the actual price received rather than assuming that a single market price will remain unchanged throughout the season.

Break-Even Thinking

A useful way to think about farm profitability is to estimate the amount of maize that must be sold to recover production costs.

A simplified break-even quantity can be calculated as:

Break-even quantity = Total costs ÷ Selling price per kg

For example, if total production costs are KSh 60,000 and the selling price is KSh 45 per kilogram:

KSh 60,000 ÷ KSh 45 = approximately 1,333 kg

Under these simplified assumptions, approximately 1,333 kg would need to be sold to cover KSh 60,000 in costs.

Any actual farm analysis should account for the costs and revenue that apply to that particular enterprise.

Profit Per Acre

Looking at profit for the entire farm is useful, but profit per acre can make comparisons between different farm sizes easier.

A simple calculation is:

Profit per acre = Total profit ÷ Number of acres

For example, if a farm generates an estimated profit of KSh 30,000 from 2 acres:

KSh 30,000 ÷ 2 = KSh 15,000 per acre

Do Not Confuse Revenue With Profit

One of the most common mistakes in farm financial analysis is treating sales revenue as profit.

If a farmer sells KSh 100,000 worth of maize but spent KSh 75,000 producing and marketing it, the simplified profit is KSh 25,000, not KSh 100,000.

Measure Meaning
Revenue Money received or expected from sales
Costs Money spent on production and related activities
Profit Revenue remaining after the included costs

Keep Production Records

Good financial analysis begins with good records.

During the season, record:

  • Area planted
  • Seed purchased and cost
  • Fertilizer purchased and cost
  • Labour expenses
  • Crop protection expenses
  • Harvesting expenses
  • Transport expenses
  • Storage expenses
  • Total harvested maize
  • Quantity sold
  • Selling price received

Separating these records by field can be especially useful when a farm has several plots with different management practices.

Profit Can Change Between Seasons

A profitable season does not guarantee that the next season will produce the same financial result.

Production costs can change. Market prices can change. Weather can affect yield. Pests and diseases can affect production, and post-harvest losses can reduce the amount that can actually be sold.

For this reason, farmers should treat profit estimates as planning tools rather than guaranteed outcomes.

Yield, Cost and Price Should Be Considered Together

A useful way to analyse a maize enterprise is to look at three major questions:

  1. How much maize can the farm produce?
  2. How much does it cost to produce it?
  3. What price can the farmer realistically receive?

These questions are connected.

Increasing production can increase revenue, but additional inputs may also increase costs. A higher market price can increase revenue, but the price available to a farmer depends on the actual market and selling circumstances.

Ways to Improve Farm Financial Records

Farmers do not need complicated accounting systems to begin improving their records.

A notebook, spreadsheet or simple digital record can be enough to start tracking important information.

The key is consistency. Record expenses when they occur and update harvest and sales information as the season progresses.

Over several seasons, these records can become much more valuable because they allow the farmer to compare actual results.

Final Takeaway

Estimating maize profit is fundamentally about connecting production, price and costs.

Start with accurate records of how much maize was produced and sold. Record the price actually received, then account for the costs involved in producing and marketing the crop.

The resulting estimate can help a farmer understand the financial performance of a maize enterprise and plan future seasons more carefully.

Important:

The examples on this page are simplified illustrations. Actual farm profitability depends on local production costs, yields, selling prices, losses, taxes or fees where applicable, and other circumstances. Market prices and farm costs can change over time.
