Farm Calculators

Use these simple tools to work with farm production figures, estimate costs and understand potential revenue and profit. Enter your own figures to get an instant calculation.

Plan Your Farm Using Numbers

Good farm planning starts with understanding the numbers behind production. Seed, fertilizer, labour, land size, expected harvest and selling price can all affect the financial outcome of a farming activity.

The calculators below provide simple estimates that can help with planning. They are not a substitute for local market prices, soil testing, professional agricultural advice or actual farm records.

Tool 1

Crop Yield Calculator

Estimate production per acre using the total grain harvested and the area cultivated.

Tool 2

Farm Production Cost Calculator

Add your main production expenses to estimate the total cost of running the farm activity.

Tool 3

Farm Revenue and Profit Calculator

Estimate expected revenue and potential profit from a crop using production quantity, selling price and total production cost.

Tool 4

Cost Per Acre Calculator

Estimate your average production cost per acre using your total farm expenses and cultivated area.

How to Use the Calculators

1. Use Your Own Figures

Where possible, use figures from your farm records instead of general estimates.

2. Check Your Units

Enter production in kilograms, land in acres and monetary values in Kenyan shillings.

3. Compare Different Scenarios

You can change the selling price, production or costs to see how different assumptions affect your results.

4. Keep Farm Records

Compare your estimates with actual results after harvest so that your future planning becomes more accurate.

Example Farm Calculation

Suppose a farmer harvests 2,400 kg of maize from 2 acres. The estimated yield is:

2,400 kg ÷ 2 acres = 1,200 kg per acre

The same farmer can then estimate production costs, expected revenue and potential profit using the other calculators.

Important Note

Calculator results are estimates based entirely on the figures entered by the user. Actual farm results can differ because of weather, soil conditions, crop variety, pests, diseases, management practices, labour costs, input prices and market conditions.

Use the results as a planning aid and verify important decisions using reliable local agricultural information and current market data.
